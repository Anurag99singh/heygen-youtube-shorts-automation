# YouTube title and description

Prepare posting copy with every daily script; save it in Video Queue U (title) and V (caption/description). It is separate from the HeyGen script Doc body. The no-generated-captions rule refers to on-video subtitles, not the YouTube description.

## Title
- Aim for 40–65 characters, never exceed YouTube's 100-character limit.
- Lead with the named product or capability and a concrete benefit or surprising change.
- Make it understandable without industry jargon. Use a truthful curiosity hook.
- Avoid unsupported superlatives, fake urgency, misleading questions, all caps and promises the video cannot substantiate.

## Description / caption
Write 45–80 natural words, excluding source URL and hashtags:
1. First line: one specific, compelling hook matching the actual video.
2. Two short sentences explaining what changed and why viewers might care. Attribute company claims; qualify availability or limitations where material.
3. One relevant question inviting an opinion. Avoid generic engagement bait.
4. Original source URL.
5. Two or three relevant hashtags including #Shorts; no hashtag block or keyword stuffing.

Use plain conversational English, active verbs and varied sentence structure. Do not paste the production prompt, scene labels, workflow notes or internal IDs. Do not claim testing you did not perform. Check every factual claim against the source.

Pass U to Buffer metadata.youtube.title and V to text exactly. Require both nonempty before scheduling.

## Example shape (adapt to verified story)
Title: [Product] can now [specific useful action]

[Concrete hook tied to the feature.]
[What the tool does, attributed and qualified.] [Why that matters in an everyday workflow.]
[One useful question?]

Source: [original article URL]
#AI #[relevant topic] #Shorts
